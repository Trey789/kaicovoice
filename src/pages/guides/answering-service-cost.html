The pricing models you will meet.

Every answering service quote is built from a few parts. Knowing which parts are in yours is most of the work.

Per minute.

You pay for the time an operator spends on your calls, usually rounded up to the next minute, with a monthly bundle of minutes and a higher rate past it. A long, chatty caller costs more than a short one, and you do not control which one calls.

Per call.

A set price for each call answered, whatever its length. Simpler to read than per-minute billing, but wrong numbers, hang-ups and repeat callers can count as calls too. Ask what counts.

Flat monthly.

One price a month for the service. The bill is the same in a slow February and a busy July. This is the model Kaico uses.

Setup charges.

A one-time fee to write your script, load your services and hours, and connect your number. Some services fold it into the first month, others bill it on its own line. Ask before you sign. Kaico's is a one-time $299 for the receptionist, and Front Door has none.

Overage.

The rate you pay once you pass the minutes or calls in your bundle. It is usually higher than the bundled rate, and it is the line that surprises people on the first busy month.

Contract length.

Some services bill month to month. Others ask for a term of several months or a year, sometimes with a lower monthly rate in exchange. Read the cancellation clause before the price.

What pushes a bill up.

The quoted monthly rate is rarely the number you end up paying. These are the things that move it.

Call length. On per-minute plans, a caller who tells the whole story before getting to the point costs more than one who asks for an appointment and hangs up. Trade businesses get both kinds.

Nights, weekends and holidays. Some plans bill a higher rate for calls answered outside business hours, which is exactly when a trade business most needs the phone answered.

Booking as an add-on. Taking a message is usually the base service. Checking your calendar and booking the job is often an extra, priced per booking or as a higher tier.

Script changes. New hours, a new service, a seasonal special: on some plans every change to what the operator says is a billable request.

Seasonal spikes. A heat wave or a hail storm can double your calls for a week. On a bundled plan, that week lands in overage.
