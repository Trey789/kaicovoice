What drives the cost of an AI receptionist.

Two providers can quote the same monthly number and sell very different things. These lines are where the difference hides.

Setup work.

Somebody has to learn your business, write what the receptionist says, connect your calendar and point your phone number at it. Some providers charge for that up front. Others leave it to you, which is cheaper on the invoice and expensive on a Saturday.

Call volume.

Some plans bundle a number of minutes or calls and bill the rest as overage. Others price flat. Ask which, and ask what a storm week would do to the bill.

Calendar booking.

A receptionist that only takes messages is cheaper to build than one that checks your calendar and books the job while the caller is still on the line. The booking is where the value is, so check whether it is included or an add-on.

Who writes and maintains the script.

An AI receptionist is only as good as what it was told. Writing the script is work, and keeping it current when your hours, services or prices change is more work. Find out whose job that is.

Support.

When a caller reports a strange answer, who fixes it, and how fast? A person you can reach, or a ticket queue? That difference rarely shows on the price page and always shows in month three.

What it is allowed to do.

Answer and book? Transfer? Text? Call out? Each extra channel adds cost and, in the case of texting, consent rules. Know what you are buying before you compare prices.
